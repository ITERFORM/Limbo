## 2. The gate

Teacher forcing: the prompt, then the oracle's own greedy tokens, go through `Ref`, so a disagreement at one position
cannot change the next one. At every generated position the tool records Limbo's top-2 (ids, logits) next to the
oracle's, one JSONL line per position. Agreement means Limbo's argmax equals the oracle's greedy token.

The operator's rule: **every disagreement must sit at a position where the oracle's own top-2 gap is below 0.1 nats.**
Also reported: the largest |Δlogit| of the oracle's top-1 token, per prompt.

Notes on the two tables.

- The four positions with an oracle gap below 0.1 are `hi` 0 (0.0053), `tools` 6 (0.0591), `hi` 1 (0.0637) and `code` 4
  (0.0907). The default arithmetic agrees at three of them and flips at `hi` 1, where its own gap is 0.0008; the

  runs agree at all of them. So the gate has teeth at four near-ties only (7 positions are below 1.0, 12 below 1.5, 15 below 2.0).
- `--oracle-arith` is **not closer** to the capture at the logit level (mean |Δ| 0.45 against 0.40 for the default,
  section 3.2): the logits are a chaotic function of 8-bit activation rounding, so reproducing the oracle's rounding in
  the layers (section 3.3) does not reproduce its rounding errors in the logits. Its only job is to show the layers are equal.

  default `tools` mean |Δlogit| from 0.252 to 0.321 and the oracle-arith max |Δlogit| of `prose` from 1.42 to 2.20 and of
  `tools` from 1.03 to 2.02, and the Limbo gap of the oracle-arith `code` disagreement from 0.1726 to 0.0543; the verdict did
  not change. That is the noise floor made visible.
- The long prompt (4,974 tokens) was not run end to end (section 7).

## 3. Why the logits differ by 0.3 to 2 nats, and why that is not a bug

The task text expected 0.05-0.15 logits of drift. The measured mean is 0.32-0.44 and the maximum 0.95-2.0 per prompt, so the
first question was whether that hides a bug at position >= 1. Four independent measurements say no (3.1 to 3.4).

### 3.1 The captures themselves were made with the GPU helping

`oracle/oracle_dump.cpp` sets `n_gpu_layers` 0 and `offload_kqv` false, but leaves `op_offload` at llama.cpp's default
(`true`, `src/llama-context.cpp:4284`), and the CUDA backend offloads any matmul whose batch is at least 32
(`ggml/src/ggml-cuda/ggml-cuda.cu:183-186`, `GGML_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH` default 32). The settings line of the captures does not record it. By the code, every prompt of 32+ tokens (prose, code, tools, long)
was prefilled with GPU matmuls, and only `hi` (2 tokens) and the one-token decode steps ran on the CPU. Evidence, with
`oracle_noise` (section 8) on the same prompt and the same teacher-forced tokens (|Δ| is the difference in the logit of the
capture's top-1 token against the capture):

| prompt | oracle_noise, default context (op_offload on), one batch | same, op_offload off, one batch | same, op_offload off, one token per decode |
|---|---|---|---|
| prose | max \|Δ\| 0.0000, mean 0.0000 | max \|Δ\| 1.0244, mean 0.5680 | max \|Δ\| 1.5768, mean 0.4921 |
| code | max \|Δ\| 0.0000, mean 0.0000 | max \|Δ\| 0.8858, mean 0.2842 | max \|Δ\| 1.6133, mean 0.3386 |
| tools | max \|Δ\| 0.0000, mean 0.0000 | max \|Δ\| 1.6446, mean 0.4101 | max \|Δ\| 1.3424, mean 0.4811 |

The default context reproduces the capture to the last digit (max |Δ| 0.0000 on all 48 positions), and the same context
with `op_offload` false does not. The capture is a valid reference, but it is not a pure-CPU one, and
`bench/oracle/` should say so (open item, section 7).

### 3.3 Each layer, fed the oracle's own input, matches to 0.1%

`ref_forward --layer-gate` runs layer N on the oracle's own `l_out-(N-1)` for every position in order (so the GDN state,
the conv history and the KV cache build from the oracle's inputs) and compares the result with the oracle's
`l_out-N` on all 2048 elements. Nothing accumulates, so a structural error in one layer shows as a spike in that layer.
`rel_upd` is |Δ| / |oracle's layer update|.

Mean `rel_upd` over the 40 layers by kind (30 GDN, 10 full attention). "attention half" is l_out-(N-1) to attn_residual-N
and "MoE half" is attn_residual-N to l_out-N, each run from the oracle's own input; "whole layer" runs the layer from
l_out-(N-1), so its MoE sees Limbo's own attention output.

| run (55 / 95 / 88 positions) | part | GDN | full attention | worst single row |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| prose, **fp32** | attention half | 3.65% | 4.00% | 9.8% |
| prose, **fp32** | MoE half | 2.31% | 2.17% | 15.1% |
| prose, **fp32** | whole layer | 3.57% | 3.80% | 24.1% |
| prose, oracle-arith | attention half | 0.008% | 0.051% | 0.65% |
| prose, oracle-arith | MoE half | 0.004% | 0.003% | 0.42% |
| prose, oracle-arith | whole layer | 0.073% | 0.356% | 7.2% (a tie) |
| code, oracle-arith | attention / MoE / whole | 0.009% / 0.004% / 0.093% | 0.042% / 0.004% / 0.384% | 0.76% / 0.65% / 16.2% (a tie) |
| tools, oracle-arith | attention / MoE / whole | 0.008% / 0.004% / 0.078% | 0.051% / 0.003% / 0.442% | 0.65% / 0.68% / 15.7% (a tie) |

Output head on the oracle's last hidden state (norm + LM head over all 248,320 logits): max |Δlogit| **0.144** (relative
L2 1.3%) in fp32 (prose), **0.00003** (relative L2 1e-6) with oracle-arith on prose, 0.00002 on code and 0.00003 on tools; the
argmax is identical in every run.

Routing: of 9,285 (layer, row) pairs, **0** have a different set of 8 experts when the MoE half starts from the oracle's own
attn_residual, and 3 (0.03%) when the whole layer runs on Limbo's own attention output. Those three are the worst rows
above, and each is a tie: Limbo's 8th and 9th router probabilities are prose layer 2 row 52: 0.01326 / 0.01326 (gap 0.00%),
code layer 15 row 68: 0.01063 / 0.01063 (0.01%), tools layer 15 row 63: 0.01150 / 0.01150 (0.01%).

Reading it:

- **fp32 against oracle-arith is the whole difference**: the same layers, the same inputs, 3.6% against 0.07% mean error
  on the whole layer, and 0.144 against 0.00003 on the head. The residual 3-4% of the update in fp32 mode is the
  oracle's 8-bit rounding of every matmul input, which the exact reference does not do. The rounding is discontinuous, so a
  small error in one half grows when the next half rounds it (full-attention layers: attention half 0.05% -> whole layer
  0.36%): this is the amplifier behind sections 3.1-3.2, and it is also why the oracle disagrees with itself.
- The six-token first look with the oracle's own node dump (`llama-eval-callback`, `--cmp-dump`) agrees: with
  oracle-arith layer 0's l_out matches on all six rows to 5e-5 (the dump prints 4 decimals), in fp32 to 2e-3; end to end
  through all 40 layers 25 of 235 (layer, row) pairs already have a different expert set, and the first/last three logits of the
  last row differ by up to 0.199 (oracle-arith) and 0.111 (fp32). Outputs: `data/cmp-dump-6tok-*.txt`.
- Position dependence is measured on the long prompt (section 3.4): the error is flat from position 0 to 4,973.

## 6. Changes to `src/step.cpp` (and what they are for)

No change to the model math was needed: every difference the gate found is arithmetic noise (section 3), and the
position-0 fixes of `74ce3bc` are the whole of the structural work. What changed is additive, plus one numeric fix:

| where | change | why |
|---|---|---|
| `src/step.cpp` `f32_to_f16` | round to nearest **even**; it rounded exact ties up | the KV cache write: against the hardware `vcvtps2ph`, the old function was wrong on 32,169 of 522,240 exact ties and the new one on 0, over 200,522,240 values (`build/tmp/cpugate/f16/f16_check`, section 8) |
| `src/step.cpp` `layer_linear` | the `LIMBO_SCAN_MAP` environment switch is gone; the value-head to key-head map is `h % n_k_head` and decay/beta are per value head | the switch existed because the position-0 dump could not tell the candidates apart; the layer gate (section 3.3) can: GDN layers match at 0.07% mean `rel_upd` for the whole layer and 0.008% for the attention half, over every position, which a wrong map would not allow |
| `src/step.cpp` `moe` | sorts one expert past the cut (9 instead of 8) | so the trace can show how close the 9th expert was; the 8 chosen and their weights are unchanged |
| `include/limbo/step.hpp` + `src/step.cpp` `forward(token, pos, want_logits = true)` | `want_logits = false` skips the output head and returns `nullptr` | prefill tokens whose logits nobody reads: the head is the largest single matvec (248,320 x 2048); source-compatible |
| `Ref::set_oracle_arith(bool)` | opt-in: every matmul's activation is rounded the way llama.cpp's CPU path does (Q8_K blocks of 256 for the K-quants and i-quants, Q8_0 blocks of 32 for Q8_0/IQ4_NL, f16 for f16 weights, fp32 weights untouched), and the attention's q and softmax weights are rounded to f16 | to separate "wrong" from "different arithmetic" (section 3.3); **off by default**, the default stays the exact fp32 arithmetic |
| `Ref::embed`, `forward_layer`, `forward_layer_attn`, `forward_layer_ffn`, `head` | run one layer, one half of a layer, or the output head on a given hidden state | the layer gate; the same entry points are what a GPU kernel parity test needs |
| `Trace::all_pos`, `attn_out_pos`, `l_out_pos`, `moe_pos`, `moe_last_ids`, `moe_last_p` | per-position and per-layer activations and routing | `ref_forward --cmp-dump` and `--layer-gate`; the old fields keep their position-0 meaning |

## 7. Open

1. **The captures are not pure-CPU** (section 3.1). `oracle/oracle_dump.cpp` should set `cp.op_offload = false` (or its
   settings line should say that prefill ran on the GPU for prompts of 32+ tokens). For W1's purpose (a GPU engine
   measured against llama.cpp) the existing captures remain valid; for a *CPU* gate they cost a noise floor of
   about 0.4 nats mean, 1.6 max, per logit. The two programs that link llama.cpp (`oracle_noise.cpp`,
   `oracle_nodes.cpp`) now live in `oracle/` and build with `make oracle` (`mk/oracle.mk`), and `oracle_noise` with and without
   `--no-op-offload` is the test of any fix.
2. **The noise floor limits what a logit gate can say.** The operator's rule (disagree only where the oracle's gap is
   below 0.1) is satisfied, but llama.cpp's own CPU paths differ from each other by up to 1.6 nats in a top-1
   logit and 1.7 in a gap, so a disagreement at a gap of 1.0 would not indicate a bug either. In the 50 recorded
   positions 4 have a gap below 0.1, 7 below 1.0 and 12 below 1.5. A gate that can fail for a reason has to be the
   layer gate (section 3.3), which is noise-free enough to show a 1% structural error.
3. **The long prompt end to end (4,974 tokens) was not run**: at the measured 0.7 tokens/s it is about two hours of
   all eight cores. What was run instead is the layer gate over all 4,974 positions on three layers (section 3.4).
4. **RoPE angle accuracy at very long context.** llama.cpp builds each angle by repeated float multiplication of
   `theta_scale`; the reference uses double. They agree to 3.1e-5 rad at position 5,000 and drift to 2.6e-3 rad at
   100,000 and 6.1e-3 rad at 262,143 (computed in float32 numpy, section 8). Irrelevant to this gate, relevant to the
   W3 gate (128k and 262k): token-exactness against the oracle there needs the GPU rope to reproduce the oracle's
   float recurrence, or the gate to accept that noise.
5. The reference is slow (section 5) and its thread pool is a `std::thread` spawn per matvec. Nothing here needed
   it faster.
